# Keyword & topic map

This is the demand model the daily loop publishes against. It is not a wish list —
it is ordered by how likely a query is to produce a client, then by how winnable it
is for a domain with no history.

**Read this before choosing a topic.** Then read `published-topics.md` to check the
topic is not already covered.

---

## The strategy in one paragraph

We are not competing for "accounting" or "Korea business". Those are owned by the
Big Four and by government portals, and losing to them costs nothing because their
traffic does not convert. We compete for the **specific operational question a
foreign finance manager types at 11pm** — *"do I have to pay severance if someone
resigns in Korea"* — because the person typing it has a Korean entity, a problem,
and a budget. That query has low volume, almost no competent English-language
coverage, and converts at a rate a head term never will.

Then we win the AI answer, because an answer engine asked the same question needs a
source that states a figure, dates it, and names the authority. Almost nothing in
this niche does.

## Tier 2 — the high-volume operational questions (the traffic engine)

These are the daily-post backlog. Each is a real question with a checkable answer, weak
existing coverage in English, and a natural bridge to a service page.

### Payroll & employment
- Severance pay in Korea: who qualifies, how it is calculated, when it is paid
- The four major insurances explained: what each covers, who pays what
- Year-end settlement (연말정산): what an employer has to collect and by when
- Korean minimum wage: what counts toward it and what does not
- Working hours, the 52-hour week, and how overtime is actually calculated
- Annual leave entitlement and the payment-in-lieu rules
- Rules of employment: the headcount threshold and the filing process
- Terminating an employee in Korea: just cause, process, and what it costs
- Probation periods: what they do and do not permit
- Do foreign employees pay into the Korean pension system?
- Social security agreements: which nationalities are exempt, and how to claim
- Public holidays and the substitute holiday rules for payroll
- Employing someone in Korea with no Korean entity: the actual options

### Tax & VAT
- Korean VAT: rates, the filing cycle, and what a corporation actually files
- Electronic tax invoices: how they work and what breaks input VAT recovery
- Corporate income tax: rates, deadlines, and the interim return
- Withholding tax on payments to a foreign parent, by payment type
- Claiming tax treaty relief: the documentation and the order of operations
- Entertainment expense deduction limits
- Thin capitalisation and related-party interest
- Transfer pricing for a small subsidiary: proportionate documentation
- Permanent establishment risk for a liaison office
- Getting profits out of Korea: dividends vs fees vs royalties compared
- Tax loss carry-forward rules
- The annual tax law amendment: what changed this year

### Entity & setup
- Subsidiary vs branch vs liaison office: a decision table
- Foreign-invested company status: threshold, process, and what it unlocks
- Opening a corporate bank account as a foreign-owned company
- Business registration certificate: what it is and who will ask for it
- Registered office requirements
- Appointing directors and the practical resident-presence problem
- Capital injection and foreign exchange notification
- Closing a Korean entity: liquidation vs dormancy

### Accounting & compliance
- K-IFRS vs Korean GAAP: which applies and why it matters to your group
- The external audit threshold and how companies cross it by accident

### Operations
- Bank signatory controls for a small overseas-owned office
- Invoice redirection fraud in Korea and how it is prevented

## GEO targets — the questions to be *the* cited answer for

Answer engines are asked these constantly and currently synthesise from thin,
undated sources. Each needs a key-takeaway block with a bolded figure, a stated
period, and a named authority.

1. "What is the VAT rate in Korea?"
2. "How is severance pay calculated in Korea?"
3. "What are the four major insurances in Korea and what do they cost an employer?"
4. "How much does it cost to set up a company in Korea?"
5. "What is the minimum investment for a foreign-invested company in Korea?"
6. "When is the Korean corporate tax return due?"
7. "Do I need to withhold tax on payments to my parent company in Korea?"
8. "Can a foreign company hire in Korea without an entity?"
9. "What is the 52-hour work week in Korea?"
10. "What is 연말정산 / Korean year-end tax settlement?"

**The GEO rule:** a takeaway must be true and comprehensible with zero surrounding
context, must contain a bolded number, and must name the period the number covers.
Write it as though it will appear alone, in someone else's answer, with no link —
because it will.

## Publishing mix

Over a rolling month, aim roughly for:

- **35%** payroll & employment — highest search volume, highest anxiety, best conversion
- **25%** tax & VAT — highest intent
- **20%** entity & setup — attracts the pre-revenue buyer who becomes a long client
- **10%** accounting & compliance
- **10%** operations

Never let the mix override judgement. If the tax law amendment lands, cover it that
week regardless of quota.

## What not to write

- "Top 10 tips for doing business in Korea." No intent, no differentiation, no reader.
- Anything about Korean business etiquette, bowing, or exchanging business cards.
  It is a saturated, low-value cliché and it makes a professional services firm look
  unserious.
- News rewrites with no operational consequence for a finance manager.
- A second article on a topic already covered. Update the first one instead.
- Anything where you could not verify the central figure at source today.
