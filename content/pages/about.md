## Who we are

Bluestones BPO is an accounting, payroll and business-process outsourcing firm in Gangnam-gu,
Seoul. We work for foreign-invested companies operating in South Korea — subsidiaries, branches
and liaison offices of overseas parents.

We were established in 2020 and we do one thing: we run the finance and compliance function of a
Korean entity on behalf of the people who own it from somewhere else.

## The problem we exist for

Foreign companies rarely fail in Korea because the market defeated them. They fail on
administration.

A filing is missed because nobody knew it existed. A payroll is wrong because a foreign template
contract conflicts with the Labor Standards Act. Head office receives a Korean-language balance
sheet for two years and never asks what is in it, because asking would reveal that nobody
understands it. An office of four people has one person who controls the bank account, the
invoices and the reporting, and everyone assumes that is fine until it is not.

None of these are interesting problems. All of them are expensive.

> [!KEY] What we actually sell
> Not accounting. The ability of a head office ten time zones away to know, with confidence,
> that its Korean entity is compliant, that the numbers are right, and that nobody in the
> Seoul office can move money without an independent authorisation.

## How we work

**One team, not four vendors.** Bookkeeping, payroll, VAT and the corporate return are done by
the same people. The most common structural failure we are asked to fix is a bookkeeper and a
tax agent who each assumed the other was handling something.

**Two audiences, one ledger.** The National Tax Service gets statutory books in Korean. Your
head office gets a management pack in its own chart of accounts, currency and language. Both are
derived from the same records, so they cannot drift apart.

**Controls, provided from outside.** A three-person office cannot create segregation of duties
internally. We supply it — as bank signatory, as the party that reconciles the accounts, as the
authorisation that sits between a local invoice and a payment leaving.

**Plain answers, including inconvenient ones.** If your liaison office has started selling and
now has a permanent establishment problem, we would rather tell you in month one than write it
up in a year-end memo.

## Integrity is a constraint, not a value statement

Every professional services firm claims integrity. Ours is testable, because it costs us things:

- We tell prospective clients when they do not need us yet, and when they have outgrown us.
- We decline payments that are not supported by documentation, including when the person asking
  is the client's own country manager.
- We hand over cleanly. Your books, your files, your access. Outsourcing that is difficult to
  exit is not a service, it is a lock-in.
- We publish what we know, with the source and the date attached, on
  [our insights pages](/insights/) — and we correct it when it changes.

## Who we are not for

We would rather be clear about this than waste your time on a call.

- **Companies with no Korean entity and no intention of forming one.** You need an
  employer-of-record or a distributor, not an outsourced finance function.
- **Companies looking for the cheapest possible bookkeeper.** There are firms that will file
  something for very little money. If price is the only variable, we will not win and should
  not.
- **Purely domestic Korean businesses.** Our whole model is built around cross-border reporting
  and remote oversight. If you do not have a foreign parent, you are paying for capability you
  will not use.
