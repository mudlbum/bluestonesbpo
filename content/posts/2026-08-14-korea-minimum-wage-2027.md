## Korea's minimum wage will be 10,700 won an hour from 1 January 2027

The Ministry of Employment and Labor gazetted the 2027 minimum wage on 5 August 2026 at
10,700 won an hour. That is 380 won more than the 10,320 won in force through the end of
this year — a rise of 3.7%. The published monthly equivalent for a full-time employee is
2,236,300 won, and the rate applies uniformly: no industry carve-outs, no regional
variation, no separate figure for piece-rate workers.

The Minimum Wage Commission reached the number on 14 July 2026 after an eight-hour plenary
session, with the employers' proposal of 10,700 won carrying the final vote 17 to 11 against
the workers' 10,730 won. Objections were lodged during the statutory comment window by the
Korean Confederation of Trade Unions and by the Korea Federation of Micro Enterprise; neither
was accepted, and the deliberated figure was gazetted unchanged.

> [!KEY] 10,700 won an hour, 2,236,300 won a month, from 1 January 2027 — up 3.7% on 2026.

For most foreign-owned entities in Korea the headline is not the interesting part. Almost no
foreign subsidiary is paying professional staff at the statutory floor. What the annual
revision actually does is reset three things that are easy to get wrong: the arithmetic of
which pay elements count toward the floor, the treatment of probationers, and a notice
obligation that falls due before the year ends.

## What does the increase cost per employee?

Take an employee on a standard 40-hour week paid exactly at the floor. The monthly
conversion published by the ministry uses 209 hours — 40 contractual hours a week plus the
eight-hour weekly paid holiday entitlement, annualised across a 4.345-week average month.

| Year | Hourly | Monthly (209 hours) | Change on prior year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2025 | 10,030 won | 2,096,270 won | — |
| 2026 | 10,320 won | 2,156,880 won | +290 won/hour (+2.9%) |
| 2027 | 10,700 won | 2,236,300 won | +380 won/hour (+3.7%) |

The gross monthly step from 2026 to 2027 is 79,420 won per full-time employee at the floor,
or 953,040 won across the year. The true cost is higher than that. The employer's share of
the four major insurances is levied on the increased remuneration, and where the increase
feeds ordinary wage it also lifts overtime, night-work and holiday premiums, all of which are
calculated from it. If a meaningful share of your headcount sits at or near the minimum —
warehouse, retail floor, contact centre, kitchen — model the loaded increase rather than the
headline percentage.

> [!TIP] The floor also acts as a reference point in the wider pay structure. Employers who
> lift only the affected employees frequently find they have compressed a differential
> somewhere above, and end up renegotiating it in March.

## Which pay elements actually count toward the minimum?

This is where compliance is won or lost, and it is the part that has changed most in recent
years. The test is not "what do we pay them in total". It is whether each element falls
inside the definition of wage used for minimum wage purposes.

| Counts toward the minimum | Does not count |
| --- | --- |
| Base pay for contractual working hours | Anything not paid in currency |
| Regular bonuses paid monthly | Bonuses paid quarterly or annually |
| Cash welfare allowances — meal, transport, accommodation | Payment for hours outside contractual working hours |
| Fixed allowances tied to contractual hours | Overtime, night and holiday premiums |

The phased reform that began in 2019 has finished. In its early years only the portion of a
monthly bonus above 5% of the monthly conversion, and the portion of cash welfare allowances
above 1%, could be counted; the excluded slices shrank each year and reached zero in 2024.
From 2024 onward the whole of a regularly-paid monthly bonus and the whole of cash welfare
allowances count.

Two practical consequences follow. First, if your Korean payroll file still applies an
exclusion percentage inherited from a template written in 2020 or 2021, it is understating
your compliance position and may be triggering top-ups you do not owe. Second, periodicity
now matters more than label. A bonus of the same annual value counts in full if it is paid
monthly and not at all if it is paid quarterly — and converting a longer-cycle bonus to a
monthly one by amending the rules of employment requires the employer to hear the opinion of
the trade union or of a majority of employees first. It is not a unilateral payroll change.

> [!WARNING] The most common error we see in inherited Korean payroll setups is a meal
> allowance carried as a non-wage reimbursement. If it is a fixed monthly cash payment it is
> a welfare allowance, it counts toward the minimum, and it is also part of the base for
> several other calculations. Reimbursement of an actual receipted expense is different.

## Who is covered — and are foreign employees included?

The Minimum Wage Act applies to all businesses and workplaces employing one or more
employees. Coverage runs to workers as defined in the Labor Standards Act *regardless of
employment status or nationality*: temporary, daily and part-time employees and foreign
employees are expressly within scope.

For a foreign-invested company this matters in two situations that come up repeatedly. An
intern or trainee performing work under direction is an employee, whatever the arrangement is
called internally, and the floor applies. And an employee seconded into the Korean entity but
paid partly from an overseas payroll is still assessed against Korean law on the employment
relationship with the Korean entity — the location of the paying bank account does not move
the analysis.

The one genuine relief is narrow. Where the employment contract runs for one year or longer,
an employer may pay 90% of the minimum during the first three months of a probation period —
9,630 won an hour in 2027. Occupations classified as simple labour and publicly notified by
the Minister of Employment and Labor are excluded from the reduction altogether. A fixed-term
contract of less than a year does not qualify at all, which means the familiar "six-month
trial at a lower rate" structure is not available in Korea.

## What happens if you get it wrong?

Two mechanisms operate at once, and the first is the more expensive.

A contract term providing for less than the minimum is void as to that term, and the parties
are deemed to have agreed on the minimum instead. There is no negotiation and no defence
based on the employee having accepted the rate: the shortfall is simply owed, for as long as
it ran.

On top of that, an employer who fails to pay at least the minimum is liable to imprisonment
with labour for up to three years or a fine of up to 20 million won, and both may be imposed
concurrently. The separate duty to inform employees of the applicable minimum wage — by
posting it conspicuously or by another appropriate means, by the day before it takes effect —
carries an administrative fine of up to 1 million won if missed.

> [!DEADLINE] The 2027 notice must be given by **31 December 2026**. It is the cheapest
> obligation in Korean payroll to satisfy and one of the easiest for an inspector to check.

None of this is exotic enforcement. Underpayment claims are among the most straightforward an
employee can bring, they are filed through the ministry's civil-affairs portal, and a labour
inspector examining one file will look at the whole payroll while they are there.

## What to do before the year ends

The work is small if it is done in November and awkward if it is done in January alongside
[year-end settlement](/services/payroll-outsourcing/) and the January payroll run.

> [!ACTION] Before 31 December 2026:
> - Run every employee's hourly rate against 10,700 won using only the elements that count —
>   not gross pay
> - Check that probationers are correctly identified: contract length of one year or more,
>   within the first three months, not in a notified simple-labour occupation
> - Confirm whether any bonus or allowance sits on a quarterly cycle that would count if it
>   were monthly, and decide deliberately rather than by default
> - Post or circulate the 2027 minimum wage notice to all staff
> - Reforecast the employer social insurance cost on the higher base

> [!NOTE] If you have five employees, one pay grade and no allowances, this is a
> twenty-minute exercise and you do not need help with it. The case for outsourcing the
> question is about the third bullet — where a legacy allowance structure has been carried
> forward from a template nobody has re-read since it was written, and where the answer
> changes what you owe.

Employers who are also working through the retirement-benefit side of the same payroll should
read our note on [whether severance is owed when an employee resigns](/blog/payroll/severance-pay-korea/),
which turns on a different statute but bites the same monthly close.
