A useful weekly cash flow forecast tells the Korean office and its head office **which week could run short and what would change that outcome**. Start at cash available at the last agreed cut-off. Add receipts in their expected settlement week, subtract payments in the week cash is expected to leave the bank, and carry the closing balance forward. Keep uncertain funding and collections visible rather than hiding them in an opening balance. The thirteen weeks in this model are a practical horizon to try, not a rule or official threshold.

## Step 1: Which bank cash can the local team actually use?

Start with available balances in the entity's bank accounts. Reconcile them against the cash ledger, and put pending entries on the reconciliation rather than changing the observed balance to match the ledger. Label the cut-off in Korea time and keep the bank evidence privately. A transfer between two accounts shown in the same consolidated forecast is not a new receipt for the company; the corresponding entries should cancel at the company level when they settle. In transit, show which account still has the funds or flag the movement as pending, rather than assuming both accounts can spend it.

Keep a separate view for cash that is not available for ordinary payments. Foreign currency that has not been converted can stay on an original-currency line until there is an agreed conversion assumption. The KRW summary is for comparing pressures; it is not a promise that the banking account can make a particular payment.

> [!KEY]
> Closing available cash = opening available cash + settled or forecast incoming cash − settled or forecast outgoing cash. Only a completed period can be shown as actual.

## Step 2: Which dates belong in the spreadsheet?

Build the receipt schedule from open invoices, expected customer settlement, payment processor settlement reports where relevant, and funding confirmation. A signed order is evidence of a possible receipt, not proof of a collection date. Have the collection owner mark what the customer confirmed and what is only an estimate. On the outgoing side, use the actual payment proposal, payroll instruction, supplier terms and tax notices applicable to the entity. A forecast is not a substitute for a [Korean filing calendar](/blog/compliance/korea-tax-filing-calendar/) or for checking the dates of your own notices.

| Row | What it represents | Evidence to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening available cash | Cash usable when the week starts | Bank balance and a reconciliation |
| Customer collections | Expected bank settlements | Receivable list and owner confirmation |
| Payroll and supplier payments | Cash expected to leave the bank | Approved payment schedule or batch |
| Tax and other commitments | Payments separately confirmed by the responsible owner | Relevant notice or calendar and payment evidence |
| Parent funding | Cash arriving from the parent, with status visible | Confirmation, release instructions and expected settlement |
| Closing available cash | Opening cash plus inflows less outflows | Worksheet calculation and scenario assumptions |

A payment owed is not made less real by leaving it out of the forecast. If the exact date is unclear, show the assumed week and who will confirm it. For source numbers that change with payroll or local banking instructions, retain a restricted evidence link rather than copying staff, customer or account details into the head-office summary.

## Step 3: How do you make a delayed receipt visible?

Consider a hypothetical forecast in KRW millions. Week A opens with 50. Customer receipts of 20 are expected and payments of 55 are planned. That leaves 15 to carry into Week B. If the receipt slips, Week A closes at negative 5 in this simple model: the amount the plan does not cover, not an assertion that the bank will allow an overdraft. If the money moves to Week B in the delayed model, move the entry rather than adding a second receipt.

| Hypothetical item | Expected case | Delayed case |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Opening available cash | 50 | 50 |
| Customer receipt this week | 20 | 0 |
| Planned payments | 55 | 55 |
| Closing balance or shortfall | 15 | -5 |

> [!WARNING]
> Do not treat a transfer requested from head office as cash already in the Korean account. Show its status and the date local cash is expected to become available.

Give head office the week, amount, driver, internal decision date and person who owns the next step. The same forecast supplies the context for that decision; it does not choose how to fund the shortfall or authorize a payment. A common weakness in an otherwise tidy sheet is that the decision date is the payment date. Use the actual approval and bank lead time supplied by the people responsible, then record a date when the team still has room to respond.

## What belongs in the weekly handoff?

Keep a summary with the first week of risk, the balance at that point, the assumption that moved it, and the next internal action. Keep a detailed tab with a row ID, original currency, owner, expected settlement date, confidence and a private reference to the evidence. A local colleague may recognise the invoice or payment batch immediately; a reviewer at head office benefits from seeing which item moved and why, without opening personnel records or bank documents. If the report says there is no funding decision this week, it can still show the next large unresolved collection clearly.

Close the elapsed week using actual bank movements. Compare them with the previously saved estimate, note which differences were timing versus amount, and redate items that really moved. Avoid adjusting the prior forecast retrospectively to look accurate. Over time, a repeated difference traced to a particular customer, approval step or settlement process is more useful than a single percentage measuring forecast accuracy.

> [!ACTION]
> Before forwarding the weekly file, reconcile opening cash, check for duplicate transfers, flag unconfirmed funding, identify the earliest material gap, and add a fresh week at the far end.

If the cash ledger and bank reconciliation are already clear, a basic spreadsheet may be all the team needs. If no one owns the reconciliations or approval calendar, a more elaborate forecast will not fix that.
